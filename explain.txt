STEP 1: UNDERSTANDING THE BUSINESS & ML PROBLEM
================================================================================
1. What is Liquidity Stress?
   - A customer is in "liquidity stress" when they lack sufficient immediately
     available cash or mobile-money balance to cover upcoming routine obligations
     (e.g., utility paybills, food/merchant purchases, P2P transfers).
   - A person may have historical wealth, but if cash inflows freeze and balances
     drop below obligations, they suffer liquidity failure.

2. The Machine Learning Framing:
   - Observation Window: Trailing 6 months of customer transactions (M1 to M6).
     * M1 = most recent month before observation date.
     * M6 = oldest month (6 months ago).
   - Target Horizon: 30 days forward into the future from observation date.
   - Task: Binary Probability Classification.
     * 0 = No liquidity stress in next 30 days.
     * 1 = Liquidity stress occurs in next 30 days.
   - Output: Continuous risk probability [0.0 to 1.0].

================================================================================
STEP 2: DATASET ANATOMY & EXPLORATION (EDA)
================================================================================
- Training Set (Train.csv): 40,000 rows x 184 columns.
- Test Set (Test.csv): 30,000 rows x 183 columns (unlabeled).
- Submission Target: Predict probability for all 30,000 test IDs.

- Breakdown of the 184 Raw Columns:
  1. Identifier (1 col): `ID` (Hexadecimal ID -> dropped from model input).
  2. Customer Profile (7 cols):
     - `arpu`: Average Revenue Per User.
     - `age`: Customer age in years (18 to 70).
     - `gender`: M / F.
     - `region`: Geographic region (7 categories).
     - `smartphone`: Yes / No.
     - `segment`: Value tier (Low/Medium/High Value Customer: LVC, MVC, HVC).
     - `earning_pattern`: Monthly / Weekly / Irregular Earner.
  3. Mobile-Money Activity Rate (1 col):
     - `x_90_d_activity_rate`: Proportion of active mobile-money days in last 90 days.
  4. Daily Average Balances (6 cols):
     - `m1_daily_avg_bal` to `m6_daily_avg_bal`.
  5. Behavioral Transactions (168 cols):
     - 7 Transaction Channels:
       a. `paybill`: Utility / bill payments.
       b. `merchantpay`: Merchant retail purchases.
       c. `transfer_from_bank`: Inflows from linked bank account.
       d. `mm_send`: Peer-to-peer mobile money sent.
       e. `received`: Peer-to-peer mobile money received from friends/family.
       f. `deposit`: Cash deposited via physical mobile-money agents.
       g. `withdraw`: Cash withdrawn via physical agents.
     - 4 Metrics Per Month per Channel:
       * `_volume`: Count of transactions that month.
       * `_total_value`: Sum of transacted money.
       * `_highest_amount`: Largest single transaction that month.
       * `_counterparty_count`: Unique entities interacted with (companies, merchants, banks, recipients, senders, agents).
       * (7 channels x 6 months x 4 metrics = 168 behavioural columns).
  6. Target Label (1 col): `liquidity_stress_next_30d` (Train only).

STEP 3: THE 85/15 IMBALANCE & WHY ACCURACY FAILS
================================================================================
1. The Observed Target Distribution:
   - Class 0 (No Stress): 34,000 customers (85.0%)
   - Class 1 (Stress):     6,000 customers (15.0%)

2. Why Accuracy is a Misleading Metric:
   - If a naive model predicts "0" for 100% of customers, it achieves 85% Accuracy!
   - However, it detects exactly 0% of stressed customers, making it useless for business.

3. The Proper Metrics:
   - Log Loss (Binary Cross-Entropy): Evaluates calibrated probability certainty.
     Penalizes confident wrong guesses heavily.
   - ROC-AUC: Measures how accurately the model ranks stressed customers above
     non-stressed customers across all possible decision thresholds.

================================================================================
STEP 4: DESIGNING A ZERO-LEAKAGE 5-FOLD STRATIFIED VALIDATION HARNESS
================================================================================
1. Why Stratified K-Fold?
   - Regular K-Fold could randomly allocate too few positive stress cases into a fold.
   - Stratified K-Fold guarantees that every single fold has the exact 85/15 ratio.

2. Preventing Data Leakage (Strict Fold Isolation):
   - What is Data Leakage? Using information from validation or test data during training.
   - How We Solved It:
     * Target Encoding and scaling are fit ONLY on the 80% training slice of each fold.
     * The fitted mappings are then applied to the 20% validation slice and test data.
     * No global target statistics ever leak across validation boundaries.

3. Out-Of-Fold (OOF) Evaluation:
   - OOF predictions produce an honest, unbiased assessment of leaderboard performance.

STEP 5: DOMAIN FEATURE ENGINEERING (EXPANDED TO 340+ FEATURES)
================================================================================
Raw monthly numbers are static. To detect stress, we engineered dynamic temporal patterns:

A. Linear Temporal Slopes (M6 -> M1):
   - Least-squares regression slope over 6 months ($M6 \rightarrow M1$).
   - A negative `bal_slope` directly captures declining reserves.
   - Slopes computed for net cash flow, inflows, outflows, and individual transaction volumes.

B. Net Cash Flow & Liquidity Coverage:
   - Monthly Inflow = `deposit` + `received` + `transfer_from_bank`.
   - Monthly Outflow = `withdraw` + `paybill` + `merchantpay` + `mm_send`.
   - Net Cash Flow = Inflow - Outflow.
   - Liquidity Coverage Ratio = Inflow / (Outflow + 1.0).
   - Deficit Months Count = Total months where Outflow exceeded Inflow.

C. Stress Acceleration (Second Derivative):
   - Formula: `(M1 - M2) - (M2 - M3)`.
   - Captures whether a customer's decline is speeding up in the most recent month.

D. Balance Volatility & Drawdown Distress:
   - Balance Standard Deviation (`bal_std`) and Coefficient of Variation (`bal_cv`).
   - Rock-Bottom Score: `(M1_bal - Min_bal) / (Max_bal - Min_bal + 1e-4)`.
   - Consecutive balance drop counter (how many months in a row balance fell).

E. Spending Diversity & Shannon Entropy:
   - Shannon entropy across the 4 outflow channels.
   - Stressed customers collapse spending diversity down to emergency cash withdrawals.

F. Sudden Activity Drop-Offs (Silences):
   - Flags customers who were consistently active in M2-M6 but showed 0 activity in M1.

G. Peer-Relative Benchmarks:
   - Customer ARPU and balance compared against their specific region and segment averages.

H. Composite Heuristic Stress Index:
   - Weighted composite index combining negative slope, high deficit months, low coverage,
     and dropped channels (ranks in the Top 5 most important features across models).

STEP 6: MODEL ARCHITECTURE SELECTION
================================================================================
We trained multiple complementary model families:

1. LightGBM (Light Gradient Boosting Machine):
   - Fast histogram-based leaf-wise tree growth.
   - Hyperparameters: 1200 trees, lr=0.02, num_leaves=39, colsample_bytree=0.65.
   - Top strength: Handles non-linear feature interactions and high-dimensional signals.

2. XGBoost (Extreme Gradient Boosting):
   - Depth-wise tree boosting with strong L2 regularization (`reg_lambda=2.0`).
   - Top strength: Robust against overfitting on noisy financial counters.

3. HistGradientBoostingClassifier (Scikit-Learn):
   - Native binned gradient boosting providing independent tree split decisions.

4. Random Forest (Bagging):
   - 400 randomized decision trees voting in parallel.
   - Top strength: Drastically reduces variance and provides decorrelated predictions from boosting.

5. PyTorch Deep Tabular ResMLP:
   - Multi-layer Perceptron with BatchNorm, Mish activations, Dropout, and Residual Skip Connections.
   - Top strength: Learns smooth, continuous representations that tree partitions cannot.

STEP 7: MULTI-MODEL ENSEMBLING, BLENDING & RANK OPTIMIZATION
================================================================================
Why Ensemble? Different models make different types of mistakes. Combining them lowers error.

1. SLSQP Optimal Probability Blending:
   - Uses SciPy Sequential Least Squares Programming to calculate optimal weights
     $w_1, w_2, ..., w_k$ summing to 1.0 that minimize Out-of-Fold Log Loss:
     $\min_w \text{LogLoss}(y_{true}, \sum w_i P_i)$
   - Resulting blend: ~53% LightGBM + ~44% XGBoost + ~3% PyTorch MLP.

2. Rank Averaging (ROC-AUC Optimization):
   - Converts raw probabilities into uniform percentile ranks before averaging.
   - Prevents calibration mismatch between models from hurting overall ROC-AUC ranking.

STEP 10: MASTER VIVA / PROFESSOR DEFENSE Q&A
================================================================================
Q1: What is the core business objective and why is binary classification appropriate?
A: The objective is early intervention for customers at risk of mobile-money liquidity
   stress over a 30-day forward window. Since stress is an either/or event within a
   fixed horizon, binary classification with calibrated probability output is the ideal formulation.

Q2: Why not rely on standard accuracy for evaluation?
A: The dataset has an 85% negative / 15% positive class imbalance. A trivial dummy
   model predicting zero achieves 85% accuracy but is commercially useless. We evaluate
   using Log Loss and ROC-AUC with Stratified 5-Fold Cross-Validation.

Q3: How do you guarantee that no data leakage occurred in your pipeline?
A: Preprocessing steps, such as categorical target encoding and scaling, are computed
   strictly inside the training portion of each fold. Validation and test folds are
   only transformed using pre-fitted fold statistics.

Q4: What domain features proved most impactful in your feature engineering?
A: The 6-month balance trajectory slope (`bal_slope`), the ratio of recent balance to
   historical average (`bal_recent_vs_hist_ratio`), monthly net cashflow deficits,
   and our Composite Heuristic Stress Index consistently ranked as top predictive signals.

Q5: Why combine Gradient Boosted Trees with Deep Learning Neural Networks?
A: GBDTs (LightGBM, XGBoost) excel at sharp, orthogonal partitioning on tabular data,
   while Neural Networks (PyTorch ResMLP) learn continuous manifold embeddings.
   Ensembling both via SLSQP constrained optimization cancels out individual model bias.
